# Keep essential reasoning active across contexts

## Outcome and authority

The owner asks for higher-level reasoning to remain active in project context:
AI users must recognize that their questions, knowledge, methods and operating
conditions can hide important problems, and that acknowledging a problem does
not establish that it will be addressed. These principles must survive repeated
interpretation, local adoption, continuation and execution. Subagent discovery
is one example, not the boundary of the requested improvement.

The first plan and first peer discussion overfocused on discovery dispatch.
The owner's clarification corrects that scope before normative implementation.
The final work must preserve open recognition of further missing principles,
without claiming an exhaustive list or installing an expanding ritual.
The request authorizes this discipline update.

## Acceptance and limits

- Normal entrypoints retain concise reasoning about incomplete knowledge and
  methods, context-shaped judgments, conditional agent behavior, lost meaning
  in transfer, and the gap between knowing a rule and producing its outcome.
- Missing categories can be recognized; the initial principles are not an
  exhaustive model or a per-task checklist. Routine settled work stays direct.
- A recognized gap keeps its actual evidence and disposition until addressed;
  writing a rule or assigning an owner cannot imply future resolution.
- A discovery assignment preserves goals, authority and evidence while leaving
  unknown problem categories open. Narrow verification remains legitimate.
- Transfer keeps the reason, scope, exceptions and decision-changing behavior,
  not just wording. A downstream executor uses the local artifact without
  upstream rescue; failures identify the first changed or missing boundary.
- A known rule can fail under changed context, resources or incentives. The
  discipline investigates those mechanisms and changes the responsible surface;
  repeated failure does not automatically call for another reminder.
- The bounded trial establishes only its observed instruction use. It cannot
  establish universal superiority of open prompts, long-term reliability,
  arbitrary-depth transfer or a model's internal motives.
