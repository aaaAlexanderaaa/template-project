# Recurring Grill questions

## Cold-start summary

The maintainer asked to strengthen the project's Grill practice, explaining
that fixed templates or fixed questions can be worth revisiting regularly.
This followed a read-only discussion of the discipline through Meadows'
leverage points, especially the consequences of changing standing defaults.
The maintainer's request supports recurring questioning and their follow-up
selects a separately invocable Grill role focused on goals, assumptions, and
proposals. The particular question set and placement below are implementer
choices.

The maintainer subsequently approved a bounded revision: Grill should discover
problems important to the current owner's goals and standards, using already
supplied context and onboarding. It may challenge the task or direction and
need not supply a repair plan for a valid finding. The public repository owns
the general method; personal standards remain in their existing private source.
Revise the role, its owning section and necessary invocation cues, then extend
the existing evidence with owner-dependent and trust-signal cases. Do not add
a preference store, scoring system, onboarding system, or runtime automation.

At baseline, the governance owner routed unknowns and sequenced human decisions,
but did not supply a stable set of questions for challenging an apparently
settled proposal. The intended result is a reusable questioning practice that
can expose a weak goal, assumption, intervention, alternative, or evaluation
before it becomes a standing default, and can revisit the same questions
against later evidence.

Baseline: clean worktree at `898fcf0`. Relevant authority is
[governance](../contracts/governance-decision-boundary.md),
[execution routing](../contracts/agent-execution-discipline.md#the-harness-selects-the-smallest-executable-route),
and [verification](../contracts/development-discipline.md#verify-promised-and-observed-behavior-in-both-directions).

## Complete end state

- Governance owns a recurring core question set, its use/revisit conditions,
  and how answers feed the existing uncertainty and authority routes.
- A dedicated Grill guide provides a reusable role assignment and worked
  questioning, including standing-default effects and a clear routine task.
- The entrypoint and relevant existing review/plan surfaces make the role
  reachable without copying the question bank or adding a new agent runtime.
- Questions can produce a supported answer, investigation, a human decision,
  a bounded experiment, or continuation. Recurrence does not require repeated
  owner approval or a new document for every answer.
- The role evaluates value and taste against the current owner's supported
  standards, permits direction-level findings without repair instructions, and
  scopes trust concerns to the judgments their evidence calls into question.
- Existing personal-context, delegation and review boundaries remain at their
  owners. Independent Grill receives intent and accepted standards while
  forming its first judgment before receiving the executor's defense.

## Choice and expected effects

The alternatives were an embedded questioning workflow and a separately
invocable role; the owner selected the latter. The entrypoint gains a short
route, while the substantive questions have one existing normative owner and
the role procedure has a distinct guide. This preserves explicit selection of
the additional questioning work. Neither calling the role nor obtaining a
favorable answer creates implementation authority.

Expected benefit: important questions recur even when the proposer overlooks
them, and a later review can compare assumptions with outcomes. Plausible cost:
a long or generic interview, repeated settled questions, or a persuasive critique
without evidence. Normal authorized work without a Grill assignment is the
countercase; an assignment with a supported proposal should also close without
invented objections. The fresh consumer scenarios test those routes. Revisit
or narrow the method if actual use repeatedly adds discussion without changing
decision quality or confidence. No measured runtime benefit is claimed here.
