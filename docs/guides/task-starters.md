# Question starters from practice

A good question can expose a different problem or make the next useful action
obvious. These starters come from owner-provided historical conversations and
explicitly identified direct feedback.
Some retain a single question's shape; others combine related questions.
The source labels distinguish edited translations, adaptations and syntheses.
The short use notes are the template author's additions.

For questions addressed to an AI, [choose wording and context together](review-cheatsheet.md#choose-wording-and-context-together).
The linked examples preserve an unfamiliar reader's complaint, a complaint
to the contextual author and author self-assessment as different methods.
Use their recipient and context conditions alongside the question itself.

Pick a question that fits the current obstacle, replace its brackets and use
it as an entrypoint. Follow the answer into evidence and authorized action.
The [task CheatSheet](task-cheatsheet.md) supplies execution moves; the
[task SOP](project-operation.md#execute-one-task-from-request-to-result) and
current owners supply scope, authority and complete applicable requirements.
A simple task can proceed directly. A question about an alternative does not
authorize a redesign or automatically launch workers. Quantities such as ten
times or a thousand runs are probes to adapt to the actual scale.

## What would ten times the scope expose?

```text
Keeping [quality and ease of use] intact, what would it take to expand
[current scope] to [ten times the scope]? How much work would that require,
why, and is it reasonable? Which costs come from the underlying design?
```

Use one actual addition to ground the estimate: where must someone edit,
check, reconnect or recover? State assumptions instead of inventing precise
costs. This tests growth while retaining the qualities that matter.
Source: [H01, edited translation](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Could another agent choose a useful improvement?

```text
Does [project] have a goal another agent could use? If I asked an agent to
improve it, would it know what to improve? If I asked it to add [more entries
or features], would it know how to extend it?
```

Observe a recipient's actual proposed next action through the normal project
entrypoint. A directory inventory alone does not answer this question.
For an isolated use experiment, give the recipient its real task inputs and
record what context it actually inherited.
Source: [H02, edited translation](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Why should this reader spend attention here?

```text
Why should [reader] spend attention on [output]? Who exactly is it for?
What do they want and need from it? What useful understanding, judgment,
action or experience does it give them? How much useful meaning does each
part deliver for the reading effort? What would removing a part lose?
Revise the actual output using those answers.
```

Inspect the opening and a demanding passage. For a template review, the owner
needs a usability judgment and repair priorities; an auditor may need the
complete probe record. Put the judgment where the owner can find it and link
the full evidence. Try removing repetition, while retaining an example or
qualification when its loss makes the result harder to understand or use.
An imagined reader's reaction is a hypothesis; the edited artifact shows what
changed. The [language owner](../README.md#language-style) governs the standard.
Source: Direct owner feedback, 2026-10-29; edited translation of the attention,
audience and information-density questions. The procedure and example are
author additions.

## What does the reader come for and leave with?

```text
What have you promised? What expectation would I arrive with, what discovery
would I leave with, and does the experience between them flow?
```

Follow the actual entrance and next action. For a guide, this may be a reader
trying its command; for an article, it may be the distinction the reader can
now explain. A simulated reader supplies an observation about that trial,
not evidence of real audience adoption.
Source: [H03, translated excerpt](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## What does this add beyond its sources?

```text
If reading one or two of our sources gives a better understanding than
reading [our explanation], what does our explanation contribute? Have we
actually compared them?
```

Compare the same reader need. A useful synthesis may connect sources, expose
a disagreement or remove prerequisite work. Retain what earns its place;
direct readers to an excellent existing explanation when that serves them.
Source: [H04, edited translation of one passage](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Did the new material change the thinking?

```text
What new input has this work brought into [the project]? Did a good source
change the structure and thinking of [the work], or did it only become one
more quotation?
```

Show a specific changed claim, question, structure or operation and what the
new material contributes. Good evidence may also confirm the current design;
newness alone is no reason to replace it. This is useful when research keeps
adding references while the resulting work stays the same.
Source: [H05, adaptation of a passage](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## What do these results reveal about each other?

```text
Put these [drafts or results] side by side. Use each one's strengths to ask
whether the others have them, and each one's weaknesses to ask whether the
others share them.
```

Inspect a useful difference and a shared failure. A unique insight need not
become a required field in every result, and repetition need not be erased
when the material warrants it. The move is comparison, not making all outputs
identical. [Review experiments](review-cheatsheet.md) offer related variants.
Source: [H06, edited translation](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Can the design carry a real case?

```text
Take a problem we have actually encountered and run it through [design].
Who receives it, decides, acts, checks the result and recovers from failure?
Where does it get stuck? What must the design still answer to work?
```

For a workflow, follow one finding through assignment, correction and actual
verification. Include handoffs, waiting and concurrent decisions when they
determine its outcome. A paper trace can expose a missing owner; a permitted
trial supplies evidence when the unresolved property needs working integration.
Source: [H07 + H08, synthesis](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## What if the current roles are the wrong starting point?

```text
For [project purpose and actual work], which responsibilities need a standing
role and which can be assigned on demand? Start without assuming the current
roles are the right design. Are we designing responsibilities or just
renaming scheduled tasks?
```

Follow an important responsibility into both arrangements and compare the
coordination and recovery work. This is useful when retuning schedules leaves
the same ownership problem. Apply the same premise-removal move to another
design only when evidence gives a reason to question that premise.
Source: [H09 + H10, synthesis](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Will this task still help after repeated use?

```text
Can [this task design] support a thousand runs? What would it keep doing, and
how would it move on when the original example is no longer the useful problem?
```

Trace an early run, a later run after the seed problem is solved, and a changed
condition. A task that perpetually researches its first example has confused
its example with its purpose. This is a design probe, not a request to run a
thousand jobs or to give a bounded assignment indefinite life.
Source: [H11, adaptation](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## What else shares the defect found in the sample?

```text
A sample exposed [problem]. What deeper issue did that reveal, and does
fixing only the sampled case leave it elsewhere in the collection?
```

Identify the mechanism and inspect the items that could share it. Keep the
scope of the evidence explicit: finding one defect justifies investigation,
not a claim that every item is defective. Repairing one item supplies evidence
about that item. The [diagnosis move](task-cheatsheet.md#find-the-first-failing-boundary)
helps test the cause before expanding a repair.
Source: [H12, adaptation](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Who already does this well?

```text
If the result we want is clear but we do not know how to achieve it, who has
already shown a good way? Find relevant examples or working methods, judge
what makes them good, and try the useful part in our situation.
```

Search for the task and method, not just more facts about the topic. Use the
[research guide](research.md) to assess the examples; a popular technique can
still fit poorly. Carry the adopted method's conditions with the trial.
Source: [H13 + H14, synthesis](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Did the next task use what we learned?

```text
Look at a repeated operation in [actual work]. Did we turn its useful
experience into a reusable method, or use the method document as a task log?
Was that experience used the next time, and which recurring friction remains?
```

Follow the next real invocation from its normal doorway. If it rediscovers
the same command or repeats the same failed access route, fix the relevant
interface or locator before writing another retrospective. The
[experience move](task-cheatsheet.md#make-experience-change-the-next-task)
describes the investigation and reuse evidence.
Source: [H15 + H16, synthesis](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## What is better now?

```text
Is [the system] getting better? What can we observe? Which problems are
solved, which no longer occur, and which still need sustained work?
```

Name the useful change and its evidence. Include the observation span for a
recurrence claim. A successful bounded task can finish while the project has
further opportunities; a required result still missing means continue that
authorized work. See [outcome-based closure](task-cheatsheet.md#judge-against-the-original-outcome).
Source: [H17, edited translation](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md#source-selections).

## Source and use limits

The [transfer record](../evidence/2026-10-29-task-method-transfer.md) explains
historical-source coverage, record identities, transformations, exclusions and
observed use. The dated direct-feedback starter above extends that collection.
These are optional questions extracted from experience. Evaluate them by the
investigation or action they enable on a fitting task. The collection is usable
with the current project's own inputs; private history stays at its source.
