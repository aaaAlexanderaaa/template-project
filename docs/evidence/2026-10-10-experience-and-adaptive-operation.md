## Scale reasoning without a throughput claim

For equal-sized records appended one per run, reading all accumulated history
on every run costs `sum(range(1, 1001)) = 500500` record reads over 1,000 runs.
Reading one new record per run costs 1,000 before indexing, retrieval,
revalidation and whole-result review. This calculation motivates separating
active state and retrievable history; it is not a measured speedup.
Under a hypothetical independent per-run fault probability of 0.001,
`1 - (1 - 0.001) ** 1000` is about 0.6323. Real errors may be correlated and no
source fault probability was measured. The consequence is to reason about
recovery across repeated execution, not promise a flawless single pass scaled up.

Reproduce these illustrations with Python:

```python
runs = 1000
fault_probability = 0.001  # illustrative assumption, not observed
print(sum(range(1, runs + 1)), runs)
print(1 - (1 - fault_probability) ** runs)
```

Neither calculation varies concurrency. Higher execution rate separately needs
contention, quota, readiness and integration observations; a slower schedule
alone does not remove a failure that occurs on the Nth execution.
